# WOMATE Canopy — Learner-facing course voice (2027 onward)

All new learner-facing lessons for She Leads 2027 and subsequent WOMATE courses should use direct, natural **second-person** narration.

- Write **you**, **your**, **try**, **notice**, **think**, **choose**, **ask** and **do** rather than repeatedly describing "participants", "learners" or "the student" from outside the learning experience.
- Use clear, warm, credible language appropriate for young and emerging African women leaders. Respect their existing experience and agency.
- Prefer grounded examples and achievable actions over generic motivational language, abstract jargon or self-referential lines like "this module reframes".
- Explain concepts accurately, with nuance when claims or evidence are uncertain. A conversational tone should not sacrifice instructional quality.
- Preserve third-person narration when objectively discussing other people, a case study, research findings or an attributed quotation. Second-person is the default for guidance directed to the learner, not a blanket ban on describing the world.
- Keep lesson text concise and human; avoid filler, unnecessary cards and artificial enthusiasm.
- Example: "You can use the skills you already have to take meaningful climate action" instead of "Participants may contribute to action across numerous sectors."
